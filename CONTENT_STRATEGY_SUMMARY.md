## THE 5 CONTENT PILLARS

**What to write (organized by value & difficulty):**

### Pillar 1: "How To Fill [Form]" Guides (25% of traffic)
- **Audience:** Users ready to file their form
- **Count:** 5-7 cornerstone articles
- **Examples:** "How to Fill I-130", "How to Fill I-485", "How to Fill N-400"
- **Traffic Value:** High (80K searches for I-130 alone)
- **Conversion:** 1.5-2.0% (users ready to buy)

### Pillar 2: Problem-Solving Content (35% of traffic)
- **Audience:** Users worried about mistakes/rejection
- **Count:** 20-25 articles
- **Topics:** Common mistakes, RFE reasons, red flags, processing times
- **Traffic Value:** Very high (low competition, high intent)
- **Conversion:** 2.0-3.0% (urgent pain = ready to buy)

### Pillar 3: Comparison Pages (15% of traffic)
- **Audience:** Users deciding "lawyer vs DIY vs software"
- **Count:** 5-7 pages
- **Topics:** "DIY vs Lawyer", "Software Comparison", "When to Hire Attorney"
- **Traffic Value:** Medium (lower volume, highest conversion)
- **Conversion:** 3-5% (decision point)

### Pillar 4: Case Studies & Social Proof (10% of traffic)
- **Audience:** Skeptical users wanting proof
- **Count:** 10-15 success stories
- **Format:** 600-word user stories (anonymized)
- **Traffic Value:** Lower (low search volume, high trust)
- **Conversion:** 4-6% (social proof = immediate conversion)

### Pillar 5: Authority & FAQ Content (15% of traffic)
- **Audience:** Education-phase users
- **Count:** 1-2 master FAQ + 8-10 topic-specific FAQs
- **Topics:** "How US Immigration Works", "Visa Categories", "Green Card vs Visa"
- **Traffic Value:** Medium (evergreen, builds authority)
- **Conversion:** 0.5-1.0% (learning phase, not buying yet)

## RED FLAGS TO AVOID

**DON'T:**
- Write salesy articles ("Try our amazing tool!")
- Exaggerate tool benefits ("99% approval rate")
- Recommend tool for cases where it shouldn't be used
- Ignore user feedback on article accuracy
- Publish before SEO optimization (wasted traffic)
- Focus only on tool promotion (content should solve real problems)

**DO:**
- Write to solve user problems first (tool second)
- Cite USCIS sources and government data
- Be honest about tool limitations
- Recommend lawyers when appropriate
- Update content regularly (keep processing times current)
- Answer real questions from forums/Reddit

## WHY THIS WORKS

**Immigration form market characteristics:**
- **Evergreen demand:** 200K+ monthly searches (people always need immigration help)
- **High willingness to pay:** Users pay $300+/hr for lawyers; $29 feels cheap
- **Low competition (for organic):** Few websites tackle "how to fill forms" with depth
- **Diaspora word-of-mouth:** Immigrants share tools virally in communities (WhatsApp, WeChat, Facebook groups)
- **Seasonal urgency:** Green card interviews, citizenship deadlines = panic mode = ready to buy

**Content strategy advantages:**
- **Zero CAC:** Organic traffic is free (no advertising spend)
- **Compound value:** Each article supports existing articles (topical authority)
- **Long content lifespan:** Immigration guides don't become stale (processing times updated 1x/year)
- **Defensible moat:** Hard to replicate 70+ articles of quality research
- **Brand trust:** Educational content builds credibility better than ads
